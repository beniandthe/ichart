## Product Contract

The recognizer may render a chord without review only when the existing primary pipeline produces a supported, high-confidence read and a separate trust gate corroborates it. If the evidence is incomplete or unstable, the same candidates remain available, but the user must review the result before it can become chart data.

This contract applies to both Rhythm Section and Simple Chord Sheet charts. Both styles use the same `LeadSheetCanvasHostView` recognition path and the same maximum-trust recognizer.

## Implemented Pipeline

1. The existing stroke clustering, glyph ranking, semantic contextualization, candidate composition, chord grammar, and compendium matching remain the primary recognizer.
2. The accepted glyph path is retained as structured evidence instead of reconstructing it from candidate text.
3. A candidate that the primary policy considers trusted must also have at least two independent symbol-ledger support signals.
4. Ambiguous 7/9 geometry, competing alteration reads, and competing round/triangle quality reads are demoted to review.
5. Native Pencil geometry at the user's written size is always the primary semantic read. Scale-normalized copies are recovery suggestions only; they cannot silently replace stronger native-size evidence. Recovery copies are quantized to one millionth of a point so a scale-and-place round trip cannot change a read because of floating-point residue.
6. A candidate must remain the same trusted chord after deterministic point-density normalization, 10 percent scale reduction, and small clockwise and counterclockwise rotations.
7. Plain one-stroke `C` and `G` reads are additionally rejected when the gesture has implausible root geometry. This closes the observed case where a wide zigzag or dense cancellation scribble was stable enough to pass every perturbation probe while still being confidently wrong.
8. Absolute cross-stroke chronology is retained from PencilKit and used as supporting ownership evidence. A hard pause can protect a new chord target, while a short pause may continue a modifier; timing never invents a root by itself.
9. Sequential row grouping restores original stroke order inside every target and can reconstruct a fragmented first root before a later root exists. This prevents a split handwritten `A`, for example, from being discarded and then absorbed into a later `C`.
10. A failed trust probe does not discard the candidate. It changes the action from trusted render to explicit confirmation and supplies a musician-readable reason.
11. Draft-preview recognition now carries the full trust decision into `Review & Render`. An uncertain preview cannot bypass confirmation when a batch is rendered.
12. Reviewed draft choices flow back into the existing atomic draft-and-barline commit path. Failed review or unsupported text leaves the ink and chart unchanged.
13. Correction memory learns a wrong-read/right-read pair only from an explicit correction to a rendered ink chord. Deleting a chord is no longer treated as evidence that recognition was wrong, and legacy deletion-derived rejection rules are ignored.
14. Gesture-template normalization is cached behind a lock. This removes repeated template preparation without changing ranking behavior.
15. The four independent robustness probes now run concurrently for the production value recognizer. Their transformations, candidate search, ordered failure precedence, and required-probe count are unchanged. Injected test recognizers remain serial because stateful test doubles have no concurrency contract.
16. Candidate enumeration preserves the full 4,096-sequence safety budget. When a prefix's complete Cartesian product fits that budget, the composer generates it directly and deterministically reduces duplicate text instead of sorting every intermediate beam. Truncated searches retain the established beam algorithm exactly, including its floating-point accumulation, ordering, and cutoff semantics.
17. Below-threshold recovery candidates now live in a separate, bounded `reviewCandidateScores` lane. They may appear as explicit choices when the primary recognizer has insufficient evidence, but they are never fed back into the automatic-trust policy. Native- and scale-recovery suggestions share the same four-item cap. This structural separation fixed a density-normalized case where an experimental fallback could promote an uncorroborated `F#-7` read.
18. Recognition traces split reset sessions correctly. Privacy-safe telemetry now records trust outcome, probe count, support count, review-candidate count, validation time, and pipeline version without chord text or stroke content. Device-only diagnostics may retain the bounded review suggestions for exact local replay.
19. Every new device diagnostic event and ink-originated committed chord is stamped with the current pipeline identity. V4 introduced structurally separate review-only recovery, v5 identifies the compact-sharp evidence rule, v6 identifies real-row grouping and root/extension repairs, and v7 identifies broader temporal construction ownership. V8 (`maximum-trust-v8-2026-09-12`) identifies the fresh D/9, circle/triangle, and later detached-construction repairs and is the preceding installed candidate. V9 identifies inset-D-stem and completed raised-sharp repairs; V10 identifies the later short inset-stem/completed-D-contour repair; V11 identifies compact-leading-root/owned-content-band construction ownership; V12 identifies root-relative suspended-middle context. The current installed V13 (`maximum-trust-v13-2026-09-12`) also identifies bounded review-only recovery for compact multi-glyph no-reads with clear root evidence. Traces from an earlier pipeline cannot be mistaken for current-candidate acceptance evidence. Legacy trace events and chords decode with no version instead of being falsely attributed to the current recognizer. Typed and manually entered chords also remain unversioned.
20. A learned correction may auto-reuse a candidate only for the same candidate signature and the exact previously corrected recognition geometry. New rules hash a canonical, versioned representation of ordered stroke coordinates and relative timing rather than PencilKit's serialized archive bytes. The correction therefore survives harmless PencilKit metadata reserialization, while a different fresh drawing with the same top-three candidates remains in review instead of inheriting a broad historical rewrite. Existing unversioned archive digests remain readable when their original bytes are unchanged.
21. Recognition-result caching now keys on the exact input the recognizer consumes—ordered stroke geometry and timing plus recognition options—instead of PencilKit archive bytes. Harmless archive metadata rewrites can therefore reuse an unchanged result, while different strokes can never inherit a result merely because their serialized bytes were supplied incorrectly or collided at the request layer.
22. Confirmation still presents at most three one-tap choices, but those slots no longer expose three near-duplicate high scores while hiding a musically plausible recovery. The native primary read remains first, the strongest independent alternative is second, and the third slot prefers the closest same-root chord structure by quality, extensions, alterations, and slash bass before considering simplicity and score. This ordering is confined to explicit review; it cannot change the primary read or grant automatic trust.
23. PencilKit adaptation now applies each persisted `PKStroke.transform` to every sampled path location before recognition. A visually moved or reprojected stroke is therefore targeted and recognized where the musician sees it, rather than at its stale pre-transform path coordinates. A serialized PencilKit round-trip regression covers the adapter directly, and a production-preparation regression targets transformed ink in a fourth Simple Chord Sheet continuation lane and a late committed Rhythm Section system.
24. PencilKit bitmap erasure is now part of recognition geometry. Each `maskedPathRanges` interval is reconstructed as its own visible, unmasked fragment before transforms are applied, including fractional endpoints. Fully erased strokes contribute neither geometry nor timing. Draft preview, tap-to-confirm, direct targeting, Rhythm Section, and Simple Chord Sheet all use this same preparation path.
25. Expanded fragments retain their original-stroke ownership. Preview can recognize separate visible islands, but it refuses to classify one fragment as a removable barline when another visible fragment belongs to the same source stroke. Tap-to-confirm never removes source strokes. This prevents an erased bridge or a barline-like island from causing unrelated chord ink to disappear.
26. Drawing snapshot equality now fingerprints all six affine-transform components and the rounded masked path ranges in addition to path geometry. An interior bitmap-mask edit or an equal-bounds transform can no longer be mistaken for an unchanged drawing and skipped by persistence/recognition scheduling.
27. Every reconstructed or color-normalized stroke preserves PencilKit's `randomSeed`. Correction-memory and recognition-cache identity remain based on visible semantic geometry and timing, so harmless archive metadata changes still reuse results while a real mask or transform edit invalidates stale work.
28. A complete four-to-six-stroke sharp may be as short as 10 points while retaining the existing minimum width and crossed-line structure. The prior 12-point floor rejected the 11.6-point sharp in the current saved `Eb7(#11)` source ink even though glyph recognition ranked it as `#` with 0.990 confidence. That rejection prevented literal parenthesis wrappers from being removed and let generic composition invent `Eb7(#11)(b3)`. The narrower height repair keeps all structural gates, removes only the wrappers around the recognized alteration, and has a captured-template regression compressed to the same 11-point range.
29. Sequential grouping protects clearly detached completed roots even when a preceding `B` is taller or a new one-stroke `C`/`G` resembles a modifier. Relaxed size gates require adjacent, not cumulative, Pencil cadence and accumulated-content detachment. Near-immediate compact suffix fragments retain the older height floor, preserving the full historical archive.
30. A far-right bar/stem/body under construction remains its own pending no-read target instead of being attached to a prior trusted chord. This prevents the opening stroke of `E` from changing `G` into `G-`, and the opening stroke of `G7alt` from changing a completed `D-7` into `Dø7`. The rule preserves source-stroke coverage and does not force incomplete construction to become a recognized chord.
31. Handwritten `B` accepts the observed higher-waisted two-loop construction, and the `9` detector recognizes a closed loop completed before its trailing stem. The seven/nine trust-conflict gate applies to minor chords as well as dominant forms; unresolved evidence still requires confirmation.
32. Saved-state replay can now audit the actual serialization -> coordinate transform -> production preparation -> lane targeting -> bounded recognition route, and every stroke prefix. A final-row recognition pass alone no longer closes a live grouping or retroactive-read regression. Simple Chord Sheet layout inference can use its chord coordinate space when no notation coordinate space was saved.
33. V7 ownership separates a detached root body, body-band-overlapping stem, or opening upper bar when adjacent cadence also supports a new construction. An upper-shifted initial stem additionally requires strong vertical geometry, substantial height, far-right separation, and the slower adjacent-cadence bound; the ordinary body-band gate is not relaxed for all fragments. This keeps a whole unfinished root pending rather than repeatedly stripping only its last stroke. New temporal runs borrow the preceding root's size/content context, not its strokes, so isolating a smaller root does not discard the context needed to recognize it. A completed supported suffix or explicit slash-bass sequence can rejoin its root. A paused sharp must have bounded complete crossed-line geometry and independently accepted alteration evidence in the merged primary path. A root-sized seven lookalike cannot bypass independence when it has strong competing letter evidence. Recognized modifier-led temporal runs reuse the existing subordinate-fragment/independent-root/semantic-continuation gates; pending fallback construction does not. Repeated root letters require adjacent new-construction cadence before they can claim independence; near-immediate same-root alteration fragments remain subordinate. If initial spatial-root discovery fails but the primary pipeline supplies a supported rooted read and its accepted root glyph above the detached-root threshold, a single full-ink target is retained without granting automatic trust. A permissive slash-shaped tail must also have a supported slash glyph before it can suppress a new temporal boundary. Modifier-pause and slash-bass negatives are part of verification; hardware delivery and fresh acceptance are recorded separately below.
34. The editor does not treat explicit pending no-read buckets as proof of a root-led split. A root-led route requires a supported leading root before it can bypass the raw fragment-collapse guards. Later pending construction remains separate from that completed root; unsupported leading fragments still use the guarded lane/gap fallback. This preserves original ink while avoiding fabricated multiple-chord targets from a stem and detached bowl.
35. V8 recognizes a completed single right-side D bowl joined to a nearby left stem by its contour, rather than an upper point-count limit. A true inward waist excludes shallow double-bowl B lookalikes from the new rule and both older single-bowl recovery rules. New device captures are regression fixtures, not added glyph templates; because they guided this repair, they are not independent writer-held-out validation.
36. A completed upper loop with a descending right-side tail can supply stronger 9 evidence than the loose-curl aspect cutoff. The existing bounded loop aspect, left turn, upper-right return, and narrow trailing-tail checks remain required. A top shelf without loop return cannot receive that strong 9 evidence. Global confidence and automatic-trust guards are unchanged.
37. Private saved-state replay refines its page-size guesses by measured writing-frame residuals. The old fixed offsets missed the Rhythm Section 130-point margin and stretched 670-point saved ink onto 700 points. Both-style canvas-size regression tests now require exact saved-frame size; no product coordinate transform was changed to fix this test setup.
38. A compact closed major triangle also needs a broad approximately straight lower base and an upper apex. Bounded larger diminished loops require explicit round-area/closure evidence and no triangle base. Two captured circles are tested at native, fixed-row, and doubled scales, including the previously trusted-wrong enlarged C-major-triangle read.
39. A trailing accidental/circle incorrectly discovered as a root cannot define the next construction's baseline. Ownership uses the already-owned bounds when the discovered root is clearly not leading, compares body overlap to the smaller construction, and keeps strongly detached short initial stems pending. Modifier-led reattachment cannot undo a detached root boundary merely because C/G also ranks as 7/m. Completed explicit slash/sharp, raised literal flat alterations, and small raised 11/13 extensions retain narrowly checked continuation paths. No chord text, automatic-trust threshold, or saved drawing is rewritten by these ownership gates.
40. The complete-sharp ownership exception also requires two distinct horizontal bars to cross the same two separated vertical stems inside its bounded four-to-six-stroke window. Counting vertical/horizontal candidates alone could borrow an F's bars and temporarily treat the first three strokes of its following sharp as a completed six-stroke sharp. The exact six-chord archive row now has a named every-prefix regression; the global sharp glyph recognizer and automatic-trust thresholds are unchanged.
41. A leading root that is compact beside taller already-owned content cannot define a too-short baseline for a similarly tall new opening. Ownership uses the complete owned content band only when that content is at least 1.50 times the discovered root's height and the new construction is at least 0.75 times the content's height. Existing detachment, cadence, geometry, completed-modifier and slash-bass gates remain in force; small suffixes retain their old baseline. The fixed six-fixture regression is independent of archive insertion order.
42. The fallback middle `u` inside guarded suspended-suffix context is measured relative to its owned root at the established 40-point reference height. Enlarging a complete `sus` no longer fails only because the cup exceeds a 24-point absolute cutoff. The actual ink and clock, standalone glyph scores, lowercase-lane/minor-quality guards and trust thresholds are unchanged.
43. Alternate-scale review recovery can also audit a compact no-read only when at least three glyph groups and root confidence of at least 0.72 remain, and total height is at least 24 points. The existing five recovery heights and four-item review cap are unchanged, bounding enlargement to 2x. Supported compact primary reads and standalone sketches do not enter this new path. Recovered scores cannot replace native primary scores, confidence or match, and cannot grant automatic trust.

It showed that the slow path was not Pencil stroke capture: long chord forms spent most of their time composing thousands of candidates and then serially running four complete robustness recognitions. The final implementation keeps every trust probe and the same candidate budget, but evaluates independent probes concurrently and avoids intermediate beam sorting when a complete Cartesian product fits the budget.

The final outlier run completed all 651 fixtures in 12.386 seconds. Its worst observed fixture was `B#7(#11)` at 306.043 ms: 116.225 ms in base candidate composition and 189.244 ms in trust validation. A stable prior example, `B#7(b13)`, fell from approximately 427.7 ms to 219.4 ms. These are simulator observations and scheduling variance remains visible, so they define regression evidence rather than a promise about physical Pencil latency.

A separate search-budget experiment replayed all 651 fixtures at 4,096, 3,072, 2,048, 1,536, 1,024, 768, and 512 generated-sequence limits. Reducing the budget to 3,072 already changed five user-facing trust/confirmation outcomes; lower limits changed progressively more, and 512 made `F#7(b13)` a no-read. The production limit therefore remains 4,096. The performance repair does not buy speed by discarding valid chord paths.

### Vocabulary Evidence Boundary

The chord parser and renderer accept a wider vocabulary than the retained handwriting archive proves. Of the 651 retained real-ink fixtures, 21 contain simple slash chords, but none exercises `add`, `6/9`, `sus2`, `9sus`, minor-major-nine, or a quality/extension/alteration combined with slash bass. Those facts do not prove that a composed form will fail, but they do mean those families cannot honestly be called handwriting-proven from the current corpus.

V2 adds provisional lowercase-`d` and digit-`2` gesture templates with strict shape gates and contextual promotion. The complete recognition path now reaches the correct suggestions for constructed `Cadd2`, `F#add9`, `Bbadd11`, `Csus2`, `C6/9`, `C-6/9`, `C9sus` (displayed as `Csus9`), `C-△9`, `C6/9/E`, and `Db7(b9)/F` sequences. That integration work exposed and fixed six end-to-end defects: explicit `sus2` evidence could be overwritten by contextual `sus4`; `6/9` could be penalized as an invalid slash chord; a second slash in `C6/9/E` could incorrectly assign bass ownership to the first slash; explicit `9sus` could be selected and then trusted as `7sus`; the shortest-first Cartesian candidate walk could exhaust its safety budget before evaluating a complete long chord; and a fixed flat-five bonus could outweigh materially stronger written `9` evidence in `Db7(b9)/F`. Candidate generation now evaluates the complete written sequence first with a confidence-ranked bounded beam, retains a fixed recovery budget for shorter prefixes, and conservatively forces review whenever any sequence limit is hit. These are synthetic integration sequences, not claimed physical-musician samples.

Every candidate in an unrepresented family is forced to `Review & Render` with the explicit `insufficientCapturedFamilyEvidence` reason, even if its primary suggestion is otherwise strong. That specific musician-facing explanation takes precedence over the generic bounded-candidate warning when both safeguards apply. Representative physical-iPad samples for `add2`, `add9`, `add11`, `6/9`, `sus2`, `9sus`, minor-major-nine, and complex slash chords must be captured and replayed in both chart styles before any of those families can become silently trusted. Covered neighbors (`6`, `sus4`, `7sus`, minor-major-seven, and simple slash chords) retain their existing trust eligibility.

## Why This Remains a Hybrid System

Apple's current PencilKit contract keeps the B-spline `path` and the affine `transform` as separate stroke properties, and defines rendered bounds after applying that transform. Recognition must therefore apply the transform to sampled path locations to operate in the same geometry that the musician sees. The adapter repair follows that contract rather than inferring behavior from the saved chart alone.

Online handwriting research supports sequence models and integrated language constraints, but replacing the current recognizer without a diverse, writer-disjoint labeled corpus would trade known behavior for an uncalibrated model. The current confidence values are ranking scores, not probabilities; maximum trust therefore uses a reject option and stability evidence instead of claiming probabilistic certainty.

Structural handwriting work explicitly aligns traces to symbols for segmentation, spatial updates, and interpretable error analysis. This informs the retained stroke/target/glyph provenance and separate ownership/classification diagnosis here; it is not evidence that iChart's recognition accuracy matches mathematical-expression benchmarks. [Graph-to-Graph](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/16399), [The Return of Structural HMER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19773). The risk/coverage distinction in selective prediction also supports measuring correct trusted coverage separately from correct reviewed suggestions and manual-only no-reads. That is a design inference, not a claim that iChart uses SelectiveNet or calibrated model probabilities. [SelectiveNet](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/geifman19a.html).

## Remaining Limits

- The fixture archive is large but not a writer-disjoint population study. It cannot support a claim that every musician's handwriting will be read perfectly.
- The trust gate intentionally increases review frequency to prevent confident wrong commits. Raw destructive point thinning demonstrates the expected tradeoff: safety is retained while more entries need review or manual input.
- Simulator timing does not settle Apple Pencil feel, dense-page responsiveness, or visual presentation on the target iPad.
- The iOS 27 stroke recognizer is a future evaluation path, not a current dependency, because iChart currently supports iOS 17 and the acceptance iPad is on iPadOS 26.6.2.
- The telemetry allowlist update is deployed with the user's explicit approval, its source verified and real post-deployment events confirming correct-type end-to-end retention of the new fields. Those events identify V8, not V13 recognition acceptance; historical filtered rows remain incomplete.
- V5, V7, and V8 were signed, installed, launched, and library-preserving, but later physical tests exposed failures despite their earlier green automated gates. V9/V10 are signed local checkpoints, not installed; V10 fixes the later short-stem D but fails expanded archive ownership. V11 fixes that ownership; V12 fixes the scaled F#sus primary read. V13 also repairs compact root-led review recovery and passes its focused, archive, exact saved-ink, application and sanitizer gates plus signed/frozen device-build verification. It is now installed, launched and byte-exact library/ink/PDF/correction-memory preserving. Fresh writing/preview/render/review/erasure/dense-page/cold-relaunch acceptance in both styles remains required. Saved-ink replay and device delivery do not prove subjective latency or all live target assignment on hardware.

## Physical-iPad Acceptance Matrix

Run each category in both Rhythm Section and Simple Chord Sheet:

1. Write roots `A B C D E F G` slowly and quickly, including the prior `B/G` sequence.
   Repeat mixed-height `B C D G Eb7(#11)` and verify each completed preview stays unchanged as the next root's first bar/stem/body is written.
2. Write flats and sharps, including `Bb`, `Db`, `F#`, and slash bass such as `G/B` and `D/F#`.
3. Write `7`, `9`, `11`, and `13`, then altered forms such as `b5`, `#5`, `b9`, `#9`, `#11`, and `b13`.
4. Capture `add2`, `add9`, `add11`, `6/9`, `sus2`, `9sus`, minor-major-nine, and complex slash examples so the currently unrepresented vocabulary can be replayed rather than inferred.
5. Recreate a six-chord fourth-system row, including the current saved forms `Ab7(#5) B#7(#9) C#7(#9) Db7(b9) Eb7(#11) Gb7(b5)`, and verify that all six entries remain separate with the correct primary preview. Make the `#` inside at least one parenthesized alteration deliberately compact.
6. Write major triangle, diminished, half-diminished, suspended, altered, and chord-repeat forms.
7. Confirm that a clear corroborated read keeps the one-tap `Render Chords` path.
8. Confirm that an ambiguous read changes the action to `Review & Render`, explains what needs checking, and never commits before review.
9. Change one reviewed candidate and verify the chosen text is rendered in the original measure and lane with draft barlines preserved.
10. Correct an already rendered ink chord, write the same shape again, and verify the previous wrong read is no longer silently trusted, including after closing and reopening the chart.
11. Delete an otherwise correct chord, write it again, and verify deletion alone did not poison recognition memory.
12. Fill a page with chord ink, erase, switch chart tools, rotate the iPad, leave and reopen the chart, and verify ink remains responsive and persisted.

Physical-iPad acceptance on the final installed candidate, including both chart styles and subjective Pencil latency, remains the release gate for this pass.
