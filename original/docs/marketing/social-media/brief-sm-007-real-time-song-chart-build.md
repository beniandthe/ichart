## Audience And Goal

- Primary audience: gigging musicians, bandleaders, teachers, music directors, and weekend/private-event players.
- User problem: learning a song quickly and needing a usable chart before the gig, rehearsal, lesson, or session.
- Product truth to prove: iChart is not cleaning up a messy existing chart or listening to the song automatically. It gives the musician a fast handwritten place to build the chart, then keeps the result editable, organized, exportable, and reusable.
- Desired action: watch enough of the process to believe the workflow is real, then visit `useichart.com` or download iChart from the App Store.

## Rights And Audio Guardrail

- If the song is copyrighted and not cleared, do not rely on the recorded song audio as the final public soundtrack.
- Safe options:
  - Use an original/cleared song or rehearsal loop.
  - Use the song audio only for sync while editing, then mute or heavily lower it under voiceover.
  - Add platform-cleared music natively inside TikTok/Instagram if the account has access to an appropriate commercial-safe track.
  - Use voiceover as the main audio and keep the visual proof as the selling point.
- Do not show full private/client setlists, unreleased client material, account details, emails, or paid forum content.

## Capture Plan

### Required Sources

- [ ] iPad screen recording of the full iChart session.
- [ ] Physical camera angle showing hand, Apple Pencil, and iPad in the real workspace.
- [ ] Final chart open in iChart from top to bottom.
- [ ] Final exported PDF or PDF preview if time allows.
- [ ] Optional short talking-head intro or voice memo for natural narration.

### Sync Marker

Before starting the song, create one obvious sync moment visible in both sources:

- Say "sync" out loud while the physical camera is rolling.
- Tap the Pencil once on the iPad screen.
- Start the song after a clear 2-count pause.

If the iPad screen recording does not capture room audio, the visible Pencil tap still gives us a visual sync point.

### iPad Prep

- Turn on Do Not Disturb.
- Hide private notifications.
- Start on a blank chart with the app UI visible.
- Leave enough top/bottom space so status bars and platform captions can be cropped safely.
- Use a generic or rights-safe chart title if the song title should not be public.

### Suggested Capture Flow

| Moment | What To Capture | Edit Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00 | Blank iChart page before playback | Establish real starting point |
| 0:05 | First listen: write key, rough form, or first chord pass | Shows actual use, not staged screenshots |
| Midway | Add sections, repeats, chords, notes, and any rhythm cues by hand | Proof of chart-building speed |
| Final pass | Clean up title, sections, chord placement, or notes | Shows editability after handwriting |
| Result | Open the completed chart top to bottom | Gives evidence of a finished usable chart |
| Optional close | Export/preview PDF or project storage | Connects the chart to real gig use |

## Edit Plan

### Primary Vertical Reel/TikTok

- Length target: 35-60 seconds.
- Format: 1080x1920 vertical master.
- Structure:
  - 0-3s: hook/title over blank chart or first written chord.
  - 3-8s: real-time proof that this started from listening, not a prebuilt chart.
  - 8-35s: speed-ramped time-lapse of chart writing.
  - 35-50s: final chart scan plus one result action, such as PDF preview or project save.
  - Close: iChart logo, App Store, `useichart.com`, `@useichart`.
- Speed:
  - Keep a few natural-speed moments where the handwriting is visibly human.
  - Use 6x-12x for longer writing stretches.
  - Do not speed through the exact product action that proves the claim.

### Visual Layout

- Preferred edit: clean app screen recording as the main frame, with the physical hand/iPad shot used as a small proof insert or quick cutaway.
- Alternate edit: split-screen for the first 8-12 seconds, then full-screen app capture for readability.
- Avoid covering the chart with large caption blocks.
- Use small top labels and bottom dialogue captions only where they clarify the story.

### Stills To Pull
